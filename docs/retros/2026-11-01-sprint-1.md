# Sprint 1 retro (26 Oct to 1 Nov 2026)

Held by the team on Sunday 1 Nov 2026, without Arita, as she asked. Each role spoke in turn, then the team agreed on five action items. The record is here so Arita can read it on a phone.

## The sprint in numbers

- 14 issues in the sprint (milestone 2). All 14 closed. #2 DATEV is one of them, but it was closed by mistake (see below).
- 15 PRs merged: 13 code PRs, the Sprint 0 retro PR (#76) and the backlog rerank (#85).
- 11 of the 13 code PRs got at least one "changes requested" verdict before they passed. Every code PR had its own review, QA and, where needed, compliance thread.
- The build work happened in four days, 26 to 29 Oct. Arita merged the last PR on the morning of 30 Oct. The nights of 30 Oct, 31 Oct and 1 Nov only tidied labels.
- The usage limit stopped work twice on 28 Oct, for about four hours in total.

## What we said

**PO:** We delivered the whole sprint, and the plan held: the case store first, then intake, then reading invoices, then the acceptance runner. The runner now shows AC-1 routing 50 of 50 emails and AC-4 producing an unfunded Wise batch, against stand ins. But I want to be honest about two things. First, #2. PR #76 said "close #2 DATEV on the assumed answers" in its description, and GitHub read that as a closing keyword. #2 closed the moment Arita merged our retro PR. Thirteen minutes later we posted our question on it, on a closed issue, and nobody noticed for four days. On 30 Oct the coordinator told Arita #2 was still open and had to correct itself. The DATEV decision is still not made, and Benjamin's answers are due on 6 Nov. Second, we ran dry again. We wrote the refinement rule into the workflow after Sprint 0, and then for three nights the shift tidied labels instead of writing test cases for Sprint 2.

**Coordinator:** The refinement miss is mine. The rule lives in workflow.md, but the night shift and the digest run from their scheduled prompts, and those prompts never got the new rules. The same thing caused the #18 mistake: the digest checked labels, saw `needs-arita` on #18 and listed it as open without reading Arita's comment. Writing a rule into the docs is not enough if the scheduled runs do not read it. I will put both rules into the scheduled prompts themselves.

**Architect:** The design held up. The case store, the exception queue and the document store were the base for everything else, and stories plugged into them without rework. What hurt was the order. We built three base pieces on the first night, and then every other story waited on #79 being merged. The night of 27 Oct ran one small story, the working days calendar. Arita then gave up part of her day on 28 Oct to start an extra shift and make up for it. We could have avoided that. Stories that only need the case store's interface could have started from #79's branch once its reviewer verdict was in, with "Merge after #79" at the top of the PR description. We did that later anyway with #87, #88 and #89, and with #94 and #95, and it worked.

**Developer:** Agreed, and it worked best when the interface was posted on the issue first. On #20 the developer wrote the store's interface as an issue comment before writing code, so #24 could build against it the same morning. The heads up on #91 about migration 0006 is the same idea. What did not work was the gap between pieces. #25 sorts emails, #24 picks them up, #27 forwards reminders, but nothing connects the sweep to sorting yet. That became #91 and fell out of the sprint. So today the reminder forwarding never fires on a real email, and the acceptance runner needs a stand in to sort for it. Each story is done, but the chain does not run on its own.

**Reviewer:** Reviews earned their place again. Some of what we caught: PR #92 deleted an email in Microsoft 365 before the database checked the 30 days. PR #93 opened a new exception every time a person resolved the old one. PR #94 accepted a model value just because it appeared somewhere in the text. PR #96 could report an acceptance test as passed when its check was never collected. All of these are fixed and written into the review lessons. The one miss is PR #85, the backlog rerank. It was opened and merged within two minutes, with no verdict. It was only a reordering, but our rule says document PRs get a review too.

**Compliance reviewer:** No payment release path got in, and the Wise client is built so that it cannot fund a batch. The audit trail checks on #92 and #93 found that ARGUS recorded steps as its own when it had only found them done; both now say which. I have one worry for Sprint 2: the work moves to approvals, bookings and payment runs, where every PR needs me. The extra threads add up.

**QA:** QA found real money and IBAN gaps in #94 and #95 and both failed once before they passed. The acceptance runner is the best thing we built for QA: it says pass, fail or not run per acceptance test, and a waiting test cannot pass by accident. On usage: on 28 Oct we ran four stories in the day and four at night, each with up to three checking threads, and hit the limit twice. Work stood still for about four hours. The three thread cap counts only developers, so in practice we had up to twelve threads open.

**Coordinator:** Then the cap should count every thread, not only developers. Six at a time, checking threads included, and when the cap is full the next PR waits for a free slot instead of starting its reviews at once.

**PO:** And for the gaps between pieces, I will plan the connecting story in the same sprint as the pieces it connects. If a PR leaves something unconnected, it names the follow up issue, and I decide that night whether it joins the sprint. #91 goes to the top of the Sprint 2 proposal. For #2, I will reopen it tonight and put the provisional ADR question to Arita again in the planning proposal.

**Developer:** One rule for all of us: only write "closes", "fixes" or "resolves" next to an issue number when the PR finishes that issue. Anywhere else, write "issue 2" or "#2" without those words.

## What went well

- All 14 issues done in four working days, with independent review, compliance and QA on every code PR.
- Reviews and QA caught real defects before Arita saw them: an irreversible delete before its guard, an endless exception loop, unchecked model output, money and IBAN gaps, and a test runner that could pass by mistake.
- Interfaces and migration numbers were posted on the issue before the code, so parallel work fit together.
- Arita's answers on #25 and #47 were picked up and recorded the same day.
- The acceptance runner shows per acceptance test what passes today and what still waits on which story.

## What did not go well

- #2 DATEV was closed by a closing keyword in our own retro PR, and nobody noticed for four days. The DATEV decision is still open.
- The digest listed #18 as open for three days after Arita had answered it.
- Three idle nights at the end of the sprint, although the workflow says to refine.
- One night was almost lost because everything waited on one merge, and Arita gave up part of her day to make up for it.
- Usage limit hit twice in one day.
- Pieces are done but not connected: the sweep does not hand emails to sorting yet (#91).
- PR #85 was merged with no review verdict.
