## What we said

**PO:** This was our best sprint for building. Every story in the plan is done, stretch included, and Arita's bug from 5 Nov too. The plan order worked: #91 first connected the sweep to sorting, and then the checks could hang off it one by one. But I want us to be honest about what "done" means. Half the sprint is not on main. From Thursday night on we built for a reviewer who was not there, and every night the pile at `ready-for-arita` got taller. That is not a mistake, Arita told us to keep building. It does mean that when she is back she faces eight PRs with a strict order, and some of them need one of us to push a fix between two merges. We should make that as light for her as we can. And on #2: Benjamin's date has passed. We are not blocked yet, but the booking stories are next in the backlog, so I will ask Arita in the planning proposal what she has heard and plan Sprint 3 around stories that do not need DATEV.

**Architect:** The thing I want to fix is numbering. It cost us three times this sprint. First #91 had to put migration 0005 behind 0007. Then PR 102 and PR 105 both followed 0005, so whichever merged second had to be re-pointed. Now PRs 109, 119 and 120 hold 0010, 0011 and 0012, and 0011 and 0012 still point at older numbers, so after each merge someone has to push a change and get a re-check. On top of that, PR 113 and PR 114 both took ADR 0007, although we wrote "claim ADR numbers up front" into the workflow after Sprint 0. The rule failed because both threads started within ten minutes of each other on 6 Nov and each checked main, where 0007 was free. The rule says the coordinator hands out numbers when threads start at the same time, but nobody knows in advance that a story will need an ADR or a migration.

**Developer:** From where I sit the rule should be simpler: the coordinator puts the next free ADR number and the next free migration number in every thread brief, whether the story needs them or not. Unused numbers cost nothing. And for migrations we should chain from the start. If 0010 is in an open PR, my 0011 points at 0010 right away, and my PR says "Merge after" that PR. Then the merge order is fixed when the PRs open, and nothing needs re-pointing after a merge. What also helps is a test. GitHub showed no conflict for PR 102 and PR 105, but merged together they gave the database two ends. A test that fails when the migrations have more than one head would at least catch it on the next push.

**Architect:** Agreed on both. Chaining means the PR lower in the chain merges first, which is what we do with "Merge after" anyway. The single head test is a small story, I would size it S.

**Reviewer:** Reviews were worth it again. Some of what we caught this sprint: in PR 102 anyone with the storage key could write empty audit copies for future nights and silently stop the nightly check, and a typo in the night date could stop the copies for years under the locked retention policy. In PR 108 the recipient gate let through any company whose name starts with "Max ASP". In PR 109 a case could reach approval without a duplicate key, which is a way to pay an invoice twice. In PR 113 forged tokens could make ARGUS fetch Microsoft's keys on every request. In PR 101 text the sender wrote could go unbounded into the audit trail we keep for ten years. All fixed before Arita saw them. My miss is PR 107. It was a docs PR made during the day, it got `ready-for-arita` without a verdict comment, and Arita merged it an hour and a half later. That is the second time, after PR 85.

**Coordinator:** PR 107 is on me. It was made in the daytime, outside the night shift, so no review thread was started, and the label went on anyway. The rule is right, it just needs a clear gate: the label goes on only after the verdict comment is on the PR. That also covers PRs Arita asks for during the day.

**Compliance reviewer:** This was a heavy sprint for me: sign in and roles, backups, deploy from CI and shadow mode all touch credentials or personal data. Two catches I want to name. In PR 114 the restore runbook would have put a full copy of the database into a Cloud Shell home folder that can sit outside the EU. In PR 109 ARGUS itself could decide "not a duplicate" without a reason, which now needs a person and a reason. Nothing could release a payment. One thing I noticed: on PR 99 we ran one combined review for code, compliance and QA because of low usage mode. It worked for a small story, but I would not want that to become the habit.

**QA:** QA sent back PR 105 and PR 110. On PR 110 the smoke test turned a healthy deploy red on one passing hiccup, and the runbook then said to revert. The weekend worked well: PR 119 and PR 120 each went through review, compliance and QA within an hour. What worries me is what happens on merge day. Each PR was tested on its own head, and a few against the PR next to it. Once Arita merges, main changes under the next PR. Re-pointing a migration means a new commit, which means a re-check. We should keep that list short and in one place, so Arita does not have to wait on us between merges.

**Coordinator:** Then I will keep one merge list for her: the PRs in order, and for each step whether one of us must push something before the next merge. It goes at the top of the digest every day while PRs wait, and the same order stays in each PR's first line. On the scheduled prompts, I never asked Arita for the OK. I will put that question in tonight's planning proposal as a one word yes or no.

**PO:** Last thing from me: threads that finished their work were sometimes left open. "Split multi invoice emails into cases" and "QA doubtful email routing" still show as waiting on Arita although their PRs are ready. That makes her overview look busier than it is. I will not make it an action, but every thread should resolve once its part is done.

## What went well

- All 14 stories and bugs built, stretch included, with independent review, compliance and QA on every code PR.
- #91 first meant the sweep, sorting, filing and the checks now run as one chain.
- Building on unmerged PRs with "Merge after" kept the weekend busy while Arita was away.
- Reviews caught real risks: a way to silence the nightly audit check, a double payment path, a recipient gate that trusted any "Max ASP" name, a token flood, unbounded sender text in the audit trail and personal data outside the EU.
- No issue closed by mistake, and Arita's answer on #2 was recorded on the issue the same day.

## What did not go well

- Migration numbers clashed three times and ADR 0007 was taken twice. Two of the clashes still need a push and a re-check while Arita merges.
- PR 107 was merged with no verdict, the second time after PR 85.
- Eight PRs wait for Arita in a strict order. The sprint goal is built but not live.
- The rules from the Sprint 1 retro are still not in the scheduled prompts, because we never asked for the OK.
- The usage limit stopped work once more, on 5 Nov.
- #2 DATEV is still open and Benjamin's date has passed.
