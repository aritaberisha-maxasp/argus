# Sprint 0 retro (15 to 25 Oct 2026)

Held by the team on Sunday 25 Oct 2026, without Arita, as she asked. Each role spoke in turn, then the team agreed on five action items. The record is here so Arita can read it on a phone.

## The sprint in numbers

- 9 issues in the sprint (#2 to #10). 8 closed, 1 still open: #2 DATEV, waiting on Benjamin. One extra issue (#73, red CI on main) came up and closed.
- 11 PRs merged, 1 throwaway PR closed (#69), 1 draft still open (#12, DATEV).
- Almost all the work happened in two evenings, 15 and 16 Oct. Arita merged the last three PRs on 19 Oct. From 20 to 25 Oct nothing moved, and five digests in a row said "nothing has changed".

## What we said

**PO:** We got what Sprint 0 was for. Five spikes have accepted ADRs, there is a test plan with 50 synthetic emails, a working skeleton with CI, and a ranked backlog of 50 stories that covers all 76 requirement IDs. Arita could make every decision with a yes or no, and most questions had a default, so nothing waited on her. What bothers me is the last six days. We were done on the 19th and then sat still until planning, because the rules say nothing starts before Arita approves. We could have used those nights to get Sprint 1 stories ready without writing any code. Also #18 has nine backlog questions and no answer yet. They all have defaults, but we should put them in front of Arita in planning instead of letting them sit.

**Architect:** Asking Arita for a decision and building on a stated assumption until she answers worked well. She said it herself on #3 and #4: carry on with the assumptions and we cross that bridge later. DATEV is the exception. #2 has waited ten days on Benjamin, and the analysis for him already has assumed answers D1 to D9. I think we should treat #2 the same way as the mailbox and Wise spikes: write ADR 0002 on those assumptions, mark it provisional and revise it when Benjamin answers. That needs Arita's yes, so it goes into the planning proposal as a question. Two things went wrong on my side. Two threads both picked ADR 0003 and we needed PR #68 to fix it. And when CI landed, main went red at once because the spike code from #55 was already on main and never passed lint. PR #74 fixed it, but we should have run the new checks against main before turning them on.

**Developer:** The skeleton went smoothly. The reviewer found a real bug in the requirement ID check (it rejected AC-1 to AC-8), and QA re-tested within minutes. What slowed us down was Arita's answers. She answers on GitHub, often as a quote reply on the issue, and our threads only wake on messages in the project. On #3 she had to tell us "keep up pls" because she had already answered on the issue. On PR #14 she asked what else we needed after she had already answered on #4. On PR #15 she had to tell us to apply her answer from #9. Three times in one day she had to point us at her own answers. That is the clearest thing to fix.

**Reviewer:** Reviews earned their place. On the Wise PR the reviewer and the compliance reviewer found eight blocking gaps between them: failure paths, retries, the batch read back before funding and the recipient IBAN check. On #17 there were nine points, all fixed with a check that proves each one. But the first reviews on PR #14 ran inside the author's own thread, three minutes after the PR opened. The coordinator only started separate review threads later the same evening. That rule now lives in project memory, not in our docs. The same goes for posting the verdict as a PR comment because GitHub will not let us approve. Both should be written into the workflow and the Definition of Done, so a new thread follows them without being told.

**Compliance reviewer:** Agreed. I also want the verdict comment to name the commit it covers. On #17 and #74 we did that ("Review record at ..."), on others we did not, and a push after the verdict makes it unclear what was approved. PR #68 went to Arita with no review at all. It was a two line rename, which is fine, but the DoD does not say small chores may skip review, so either we review them or we write down that we don't.

**QA:** QA evidence was strong where there was behaviour to test: #16 and #17 each failed QA once with a real gap and passed on the re-test. For document only PRs (spikes, ADRs, the backlog) we wrote "no QA evidence, docs only" each time, but the Definition of Done does not allow that. It should say so in plain words, so Arita sees the same rule every time. One more small thing: #6 was closed with `status:in-review` still on it, so the board showed a closed issue in review.

**PO:** On the idle week, my proposal is a simple rule: when no issue in the current sprint is ready, the night shift may do refinement for the next top ranked stories. QA writes test cases, the architect adds technical notes and the PO checks the Definition of Ready. No code and no branches until Arita approves the sprint. That keeps her approval rule intact and makes Monday planning faster.

**Architect:** And for ADR numbers, the author checks main and all open PRs before picking a number and states it in the PR title. When two threads start at the same time, the coordinator hands out the numbers.

## What went well

- Every question to Arita came with a default, so work never waited on her.
- Separate reviewer, compliance and QA passes caught real defects before Arita saw them.
- CI with tests, lint and a secret scan runs on every PR, and we proved it goes red (#69).
- The backlog covers every requirement ID and every acceptance test.

## What did not go well

- Threads missed Arita's answers on GitHub three times in one day.
- The first reviews ran inside the author's thread.
- Two key review rules exist only in project memory, not in the docs.
- ADR number clash (#68) and red main right after CI landed (#74).
- Six idle days at the end of the sprint, and #18 still unanswered.
- #2 DATEV has been blocked for ten days.
